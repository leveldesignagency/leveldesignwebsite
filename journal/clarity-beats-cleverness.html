Why clarity beats cleverness

The Swiss International Style solved this decades ago. High-trust sectors still need the same answer.

5 min read · Journal

← Back to articles

There is a temptation, on every new brief, to prove how much you can do. More animation. More metaphor. More visual noise dressed up as personality. It rarely survives contact with a real user who arrived with a question and thirty seconds of patience.

The Swiss International Style - Müller-Brockmann, Josef Müller-Brockmann's grids, the whole disciplined lineage - was never about being boring. It was about removing anything that did not earn its place. Typography that reads. Hierarchy that guides. Space that lets information breathe. That movement understood something we keep relearning: clarity is not the absence of creativity. It is creativity with a purpose.

Trust is a design problem

When we rebuilt Richtons' digital presence, the brief was not to impress other designers. Surveying and engineering clients need to know, quickly, that this firm is credible, capable, and easy to work with. Every decorative gesture that slowed that read was a liability.

The same logic runs through healthcare and professional services work - sites like Najib Daulatzai where patients need reassurance before they need spectacle. Clever transitions do not calm someone booking a consultation. Clear structure does.

If someone has to decode your layout, they have already lost confidence in your thinking.

Cleverness has a half-life

Memphis and postmodern play still influence us - colour, rhythm, unexpected pairings. But even the boldest movements worked because they had rules underneath. The problem is not being distinctive. It is being distinctive without a system. Clever one-offs age badly. Clear systems scale.

That is why we push back on novelty for its own sake. A strong type scale, consistent spacing, and honest copy will outperform a clever homepage every quarter of every year. The work in our In the wild gallery succeeds when the idea is visible in three seconds and the detail rewards a longer look.

What we take into every project

Start with the user's job, not the mood board. Design for scanability first, depth second. Treat words as part of the interface, not filler around it. And when in doubt, remove one element and see if anything important actually broke.

Clarity is not safe. It is difficult. It requires saying no. But when the goal is qualified enquiries, booked calls, and trust from people who do not know you yet - clarity is still the most radical choice on the table.
