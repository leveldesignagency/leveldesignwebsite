Growth

Making good work work harder

Beautiful work that nobody finds is an expensive portfolio piece. Distribution and conversion are design problems too.

6 min read · Journal

← Back to articles

There is a version of agency life that stops at launch - hand over the files, take the screenshot, move on. We are not interested in that version. If a site or product does not help a business grow, the design only solved half the brief.

Traffic is not the scoreboard

Vanity metrics flatter everyone and help no one. For construction firms, healthcare practices, and B2B services, the point is qualified enquiries - people who actually match capability and budget. That shifts what you optimise: service page structure, proof points, form friction, speed on mobile, language that sounds like the client not the agency.

SEO and AI search are structure problems

Search - traditional and AI-mediated - rewards clarity, semantic structure, and pages that answer real questions. That is not a post-launch bolt-on. It influences information architecture from the start. Headings that mean something. Internal linking that reflects how buyers think. Content that can be quoted accurately by models without losing nuance.

The best growth work does not feel like marketing. It feels like the product finally explaining itself properly.

Conversion is continuity

A strong homepage means little if the contact flow feels like a different company. Typography, tone, and interaction patterns should carry through. On professional services sites - surveying, surgery, consulting - trust accumulates in the details. Broken continuity breaks trust.

What we measure

Enquiry quality. Form completion. Time to key actions. Search visibility for terms that matter commercially. Not everything is a dashboard - sometimes the right metric is a sales team saying leads are better informed before the first call.

Good work should work harder. Design and growth are not separate acts. They are the same story told at different scales.
