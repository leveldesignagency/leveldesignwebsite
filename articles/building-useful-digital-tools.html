Building useful digital tools

AI makes it easier to ship features. It does not make it easier to ship the right ones.

6 min read · Journal

← Back to articles

The best digital tools disappear into the workflow they support. You do not admire a well-designed extension on the tenth use. You simply get the answer and move on. That bar sounds low until you try to clear it consistently across browsers, screen sizes, and users who never read onboarding.

Start with friction, not features

Nimbus began with a simple observation: reading online still means leaving the page. Dictionary tabs. Translation tools. Search rabbit holes. We designed around the highlight — definitions, AI explanations, pronunciation, translation — in a panel that respects the page underneath. The product is useful because it removes steps, not because it announces intelligence.

That constraint shaped everything. Compact UI. High contrast without shouting. Interactions that feel native to the browser rather than bolted on. Tools win when they respect context.

Platforms need a point of view

Pocdocs sits at the opposite scale — a health platform where dense PDFs needed to become mobile-friendly reading experiences. Here the job was not speed alone but comprehension. Layout, type, and progressive disclosure had to make clinical content approachable without dumbing it down.

Useful software answers a question the user already has — before they have to go looking elsewhere.

Where AI actually helps

We are skeptical of AI as decoration. Less skeptical of AI as infrastructure. In-browser explanation in Nimbus. Document conversion and layout intelligence in Pocdocs. Internal tooling on client portals. The pattern is the same: AI should reduce cognitive load at the moment of need, not add a chat box because it is fashionable.

The current wave of AI product design often mistakes verbosity for value. Users do not want a monologue. They want the right output, fast, with enough transparency to trust it. That is a UX problem as much as a model problem.

Our build checklist

Define the single action that makes the tool worth installing or returning to. Prototype that before the dashboard. Test on real content, not lorem ipsum. Design empty states and error states as carefully as the happy path. And ship something small enough that you can learn from actual use within weeks, not quarters.

Useful beats impressive. Every time.
