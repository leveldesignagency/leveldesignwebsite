The changing shape of the web

A few thoughts on designing digital experiences that still feel human — while everything underneath keeps shifting.

8 min read · Journal

← Back to articles

The web has been declared dead, reborn, and dead again more times than any of us can count. What is actually happening is less dramatic and more interesting: the surface is fragmenting while the need for trust, clarity, and craft intensifies.

From pages to answers

People still visit websites. They also ask models to summarise them. They expect interfaces to adapt, load instantly, and work on a phone in poor signal. AI has not replaced the web — it has added a layer of mediation. Your content can be read by humans and parsed by systems at the same time. Design has to account for both.

That changes what "good" looks like. Semantic structure matters. Plain language matters. Pages that can stand alone as coherent answers matter. A beautiful layout that models misread is not beautiful in practice.

Human does not mean nostalgic

Human-feeling experiences are not skeuomorphic throwbacks or hand-drawn novelty for its own sake. They are experiences that respect attention, reduce anxiety, and leave people feeling oriented rather than processed.

On OnTimely, human meant making complex event operations feel calm — clear states, forgiving flows, language that sounds like a competent colleague. On Nimbus, human meant learning tools that stay out of the way until you need them. The pattern is empathy expressed as interaction design.

The next web will not be won by who adds the most AI. It will be won by who uses it with the most judgment.

Movements as anchors, not cages

Brutalism in web design had a moment — raw structure, exposed grids, anti-corporate honesty. Swiss modernism still informs how we think about typographic order. Neither is a template to paste. They are reminders that every era reacts to what came before, and the best work steals principles while ignoring fashion.

What we are building toward

Fast, accessible sites with modern stacks. AI-assisted workflows where they reduce friction. Interfaces that convert without bullying. Brands that feel consistent whether you meet them on a poster, a portal, or a chat summary.

The shape of the web will keep changing. The need for work that feels considered, credible, and alive will not.
