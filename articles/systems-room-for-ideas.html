Good systems create room for ideas

How structure helps creative teams move faster without flattening the work.

6 min read · Journal

← Back to articles

Design systems have a reputation problem. Say the phrase and some people picture bureaucracy - component libraries that turn every product into the same grey rectangle. That is not a system. That is a cage built by people who confused consistency with conformity.

Bauhaus had it right

The Bauhaus project was radical not because it ignored rules but because it unified them. Form following function. Craft meeting industry. A shared vocabulary across disciplines so ideas could travel faster. The goal was not repetition. It was liberation from reinventing the basics on every object, poster, and building.

Digital product work needs the same discipline. Tokens for colour and type. Spacing scales that survive responsive breakpoints. Components for buttons, cards, and forms that behave predictably. Then - crucially - room to break the pattern when the story demands it.

Systems in practice

On larger builds like OnTimely or AI systems work, a lightweight system pays off immediately. Designers spend time on flows and states, not debating corner radii for the fourth time. Developers ship faster because the building blocks agree. Clients get a product that can grow without visual drift every sprint.

Structure is not the opposite of creativity. It is what stops creativity leaking into admin.

When to break the grid

Campaign work - posters, event creative, social - often needs to bend or break the system. That is healthy. Memphis designers broke modernist grids on purpose. The key is knowing what you are breaking and why. A system should document not only the defaults but the principles underneath them, so exceptions still feel related.

How we keep systems alive

Start small. Document decisions as you make them, not six months later. Tie components to use cases, not abstract atoms. Review when real content stress-tests the rules. And treat the system as a studio tool, not a client deliverable to inflate the invoice.

Good systems do not make work boring. They make the interesting parts easier to reach.
